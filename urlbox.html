The pattern

Read, then passed over

Seven recorded mentions in the historic ChatGPT corpus is genuine presence. Only two converted into a citation — and in two more, a competitor's page was footnoted for a sentence that named Urlbox.

In “what is the alternative to phantomjs?” it named Urlbox and footnoted zenrows.com, while /alternative-to/phantomjs sat in the retrieved set, uncited.

Retrieved, then dropped

3

Pages surfaced but were never used on “screenshot tool that lets you scroll”, “how to get a proxy url” and “how to generate image url for html”.

Consumer tools, MDN and php.cn took those answers instead.

Five of seven historic appearances were retrieval without citation credit. That is a different failure from invisibility: the content is being found and read, then passed over at the moment attribution is assigned. The live tests repeat it — most sharply on the compliance query, where a homepage built entirely around forensic defensibility was retrieved and then beaten by vendors most buyers have never heard of.

Source landscape

Who arbitrates the category

The sources models actually quoted across the tested answers. The first is the one worth dwelling on.

  • screenshotone.com/blog/best-screenshot-apis — a competitor's own roundup, cited as a neutral authority in two separate answers
  • scrapfly.io — supports the Urlbox reliability claim
  • screenshotapi.to — supports the Urlbox feature-depth claim
  • olostep.com — includes Urlbox, ranks page one for the category term
  • derrick-app.com — ranks first organically, leads with CaptureKit
  • thunderbit.com — tested-ten-APIs format
  • getscreenshotapi.com — used for the Urlbox price point
  • differ.blog — took the citation for a sentence naming Urlbox
  • zenrows.com — took the citation on the PhantomJS answer
  • snapshotarchive.com, pagefreezer.com, screenshotcenter.com — own the compliance-evidence answer entirely

Urlbox is present in several of these, which is why it keeps placing second rather than vanishing. What it does not have is an equivalent asset of its own earning citations — no owned comparison page surfaced in any of the five tests, while a direct competitor's did, twice.

Observations

Two things that look fixable

Stated as observations from the data, not as a strategy recommendation.

The customer evidence. Asked point-blank who uses Urlbox, the model found the 1,700-customer and 800M-screenshot figures but no named accounts, and filled the gap with guesses. The customers page and testimonials exist on the site; they were not retrieved. Competitors surfaced concrete counts and at least one named account in the same answer.

The compliance gap. Forensic defensibility is the homepage headline, and it is the one query class where Urlbox scored worst. The winning answers were built from pages naming a specific mechanism — hash certificates, UTC watermarks, court-admissible records. The Urlbox homepage asserts defensibility without a retrievable page spelling out how it is achieved.

Methodology and limits

Read this as a signal, not a score

This is a single-run qualitative sample of five queries. Each prompt was issued once, and answers from these models vary between runs, so treat the direction as reliable and any individual citation as a snapshot. The Google AI Overview returned asynchronously and its text was not captured — that surface was inferred from the organic results around it.

It is not a standard AUQ GEO audit. There is no 39-query set, no verified-citation count, and no engine-produced score or band, so the visibility figure above is deliberately blank. The scored, verified output is still pending geo-audit-full.zip and team-config.env; once the engine runs, its numbers supersede everything in this document.
